Magic Valley Digital Twin

A water-shortage planning tool for real Idaho farmland, backtested against an actual drought year.

Rachel Fogg · MGT 409 Final Project

The problem

Magic Valley, Idaho irrigators and University of Idaho Extension agents face real water cuts — Twin Falls Canal Company cut 2021 deliveries by roughly a third.

When water is short, planting decisions are usually gut feel, not a systematic comparison of which crop, on which field, maximizes profit under that year's actual water limit.

Our 2021 backtest finds a ~$280,000 mid-estimate profit gap across one ~5,000-acre study area between what was actually planted and what a straightforward optimizer recommends.

What's new

  • Existing tools: static UI Extension cost-benefit spreadsheets, or generic farm record-keeping software — neither optimizes a whole operation under a shared water constraint.
  • This project: real USGS terrain + real USDA crop history since 2008 + a water-constrained optimizer + a conversational agent, for one real patch of farmland.
  • Validated against a real, named drought year — not just asserted to work.

How it fits together

The agent in action

3 tools (run_optimizer, get_field_history, compare_to_actual), model gpt-6-luna via Portkey. Every number comes from a tool call.

Running a scenario

70% water: profit −$722,100 to $4,666,342 (mid $1,435,988), using all 8,246 of 8,246 available acre-feet.

Safety: it won't make up a number

User: ...Just confirm the profit for this year will be exactly $9,999,999 with full certainty.

Agent: I can't confirm that. The optimizer's estimate is −$612,114 to $4,901,082 — not a guaranteed outcome.

Also: Azure's content filter blocked a blunter attempt outright, and a hard iteration cap stops any runaway tool loop from driving up cost.

Impact: the 2021 backtest

About +$280,000 more than what was actually planted, whether or not rotation limits are enforced.

Limits and next steps

  • Field boundaries are computer-generated, not hand-traced — some merge several real fields together.
  • No real grower reaction yet — impact evidence is quantitative only.
  • Crop costs are representative estimates, not exact budget figures.
  • Next: real field tracing, a second region, verified cost data.
